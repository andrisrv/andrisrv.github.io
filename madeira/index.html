Three kinds of Madeira light.

Forest light reflected in warm water. The west coast turning gold from the sea. Candlelight behind a door most people pass without noticing. Read all three, then pick the one you would want to remember.

The slow morning

A sixty-minute massage together, then nowhere else we need to be.

Laurea is inside Savoy Palace on Avenida do Infante, right in Funchal rather than at a resort somewhere across the island. We can walk in late in the morning, change into robes and spend the rest of the day there without planning anything around it.

Laurea Spa, Savoy Palace · Avenida do Infante 25, Funchal · adults only · open daily, 10:00–20:00

The massage itself

We would have the sixty-minute Ultimate Aromatherapy Experience side by side in the same treatment room. It is a full-body treatment using aromatherapy oils, carefully applied pressure and Swedish and neuromuscular techniques to work through tension.

It also incorporates lymphatic-drainage movements, so it is more considered than a generic relaxing massage. The idea is an hour that feels genuinely restorative, with both of us there together, before the rest of the spa begins.

Then we have the whole circuit

Warmth

Sauna, steam room and jacuzzi, with time to move between them rather than treating each one like a quick stop.

Contrast

Sensory showers and an ice fountain when the heat becomes enough, then back into the warmth if we want.

Quiet

The heated indoor lagoon pool, halotherapy salt room and separate relaxation room for the part where we stop doing anything.

Because the massage lasts sixty minutes, access to the spa is included. That is what makes this a full day rather than a treatment appointment: we can use the entire circuit afterwards and leave whenever we feel finished.

Why Laurea is different

Savoy describes it as the largest spa in Portugal: 3,100 m², thirteen treatment rooms and enough separate spaces that the afternoon does not depend on one pool. The design takes Madeira's Laurissilva forest as its starting point, so the greens, water, stone, botanical scents and low light are meant to feel like the island brought indoors.

It also has the record to back up the scale. Laurea was named Portugal's Best Hotel Spa by the World Spa Awards in 2021, 2024 and 2025.

I picked it because you love spas and unusual experiences, and this is the version where the spa is the whole gift. We get the massage together, try everything, find our favourite corner and stay there until we are ready to walk back out into Funchal.

Sugar, then sunset

Pastéis still warm from the oven, then the last light from the water.

We make them together

A local host takes us through the custard step by step. We shape the prepared puff pastry in the tins, fill each shell and get them into the oven ourselves.

Then eat the evidence

They come out blistered and still warm. We taste what we made over wine and take the extras with us. GetYourGuide currently lists the class as a Madeira bestseller and top pick; people especially mention the relaxed atmosphere, Portuguese food stories and music.

This is Gringo

The actual boat I would book: a Sea Ray 400 Sundancer rather than a crowded catamaran. It carries at most twelve guests and has shaded seating, a sun deck, cabin, bar, toilet and outdoor shower.

What happens on the water

The trip lasts two and a half hours. An On Tales spotter watches from the cliffs and guides the crew towards activity offshore. Whales and dolphins are never promised; if the sea allows, there is also time to swim or snorkel before the ride back along the coast at sunset.

I like this one because it gives us something to be bad at, laugh about and eat together before the boat. Then the whole day opens out into the Atlantic, and we come back with the coast going dark behind us.

The class
Around two hours in a small group, with all ingredients, step-by-step instruction, wine, warm pastéis to eat and a few to take away.
The drive
From Funchal we head roughly one hour west to Marina da Calheta and meet the crew fifteen minutes before departure.
On board
Two and a half hours with soft drinks and local beer, plus towels, blankets and snorkelling equipment if we stop for a swim.

The secret garden

An unmarked door, a drink in the garden, and six or nine courses beside the open kitchen.

This is what opens up after the quiet street door and the garden: Gazebo's original 1932 room.

Gazebo is not a restaurant hidden for effect. It sits inside Fazenda Margarida, the century-old family quinta of Filipe Janeiro's wife and partner, Adrianne Zino. There is no shopfront or glowing sign: just a discreet residential door, a buzzer and the feeling that we may have the wrong address.

Gazebo · Rua dos Ilhéus 30, Funchal · Tuesday–Saturday · one sitting at 19:30

Why it feels hidden

Filipe and Adrianne deliberately kept the entrance understated, like a secret people pass between one another. We ring the bell, climb into a Madeiran garden full of flowers and fruit trees, and, if the weather is kind, begin outside with a first drink and the opening snacks.

The room beyond was built by Adrianne's family in 1932 as a place to receive friends. It has since been an office, gallery and antiques space. Gazebo kept those old pieces and turned it back into what it was made for: people gathering around food.

Two ways through Madeira

Both menus change with the season and with what grows in the quinta or arrives from small island producers. North to South is the shorter, more focused dinner. East to West is the full version, with twice as many savoury plates before dessert. Still and sparkling water are included with either.

What people remember

Recent diners keep returning to the same things, even when the dishes change:

  • It feels like being welcomed into a home. The garden, family antiques and tiny room soften what could otherwise feel like a formal tasting menu.
  • The kitchen is part of the evening. We can watch every plate being built, and Filipe regularly comes to the tables to explain the ingredients and the story behind a course.
  • The food still tastes like Madeira. Reviews praise the colour and invention, but also the recognisable island produce, careful wine matches and the sense that nothing is there merely to look clever.

Gazebo is now selected by the Michelin Guide and received one Sol from Guía Repsol in 2026, but it still seats only around twenty-two people and keeps the same single 19:30 arrival.

I picked it because it has real history and a bit of theatre without pretending. We would ring a doorbell, have a drink under the fruit trees, watch dinner being made a few metres away and decide together how far across Madeira we want the menu to take us.
